How the challenge grows

Build chronology one person at a time

A Timeline game begins with one historical figure already placed. Every new card must be inserted before, after or between the people already on the line. The birth year is revealed only after the card is placed correctly.

1. Start with an anchor

The first person gives you a known point in time. Their revealed birth year becomes the first reference for everything that follows.

2. Place the next person

Use the portrait, name and birthplace to decide where the new person belongs in relation to the existing timeline.

3. Let the line teach you

Every successful placement adds another dated reference point, making the timeline richer and the remaining choices more precise.

What it trains

Relative chronology

Timeline focuses on the order of history rather than exact memorisation. Knowing that one person belongs between two others is already useful historical knowledge, even if you could not recall the precise year.

As the line fills up, the game also makes historical overlap visible: artists, rulers, scientists and political leaders who are often learned separately can suddenly appear as near-contemporaries.
