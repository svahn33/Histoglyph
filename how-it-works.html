How Histoglyph works

Three ways to test the same historical knowledge

Histoglyph uses people as the common thread between geography, chronology and visual recognition. The same collection can therefore feel very different depending on the game mode you choose.

Life & Death Map

Identify a life from where it began and ended

The map game presents two years and two locations: the person's birth and death. You use those clues together to identify the historical figure. A map can make an otherwise familiar biography feel different because it asks where events belong, not just when they happened.

Untimed play is the default. A timed option adds a twenty-second countdown and scoring. Place names can be shown before the guess, and an occupation hint is available during a round when you need another clue.

Timeline

Place people in chronological order

Timeline starts with one person already placed. The next card must go before, after or between the people on the line. Once a placement is correct, the birth year is revealed and the card becomes a permanent reference point for the rest of that game.

The challenge becomes more precise as the timeline grows. Early in a game you may only decide whether someone belongs before or after a single person; later you may need to choose between several neighbouring lives.

Choose a Timeline collection →

Portrait

Recognise the person before reading the caption

Portrait reverses the usual museum or textbook experience. Instead of seeing a portrait next to a name, you first see the image and try to recall the person. By default there is no other information.

If the image is not enough, three clue types can be revealed: life years, occupation, and birth/death places. These clues can also be enabled from the start in the game settings for a more guided version.

Choose a Portrait collection →

Collections

Choose the historical field before choosing the challenge

A collection defines the pool of people. World History is broad, while a focused collection such as American Presidents limits the game to a particular theme. The same collection can be used across different game modes because the underlying people and historical data are shared.

Difficulty and birth-year filters narrow the pool further. A birth-year range of 1850–1950, for example, only allows people born within that interval. “Include all” plays every person left after the selected filters are applied.

Answers & learning

The aim is recall, not perfect spelling

Where a game asks for a name, Histoglyph accepts several practical answer forms, including recognised surname-only answers and selected shortened forms. An incorrect guess normally lets you try again instead of immediately ending the round.

The games are deliberately short and repeatable. A person seen on the map may later appear in Timeline or Portrait, giving you another way to recall the same historical record.
